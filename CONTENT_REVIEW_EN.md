## Primary research question

How can the wave-function structure of noninteracting electrons shape correlated physics?

## Home introduction

I study the topology and quantum geometry of electronic bands. A question guiding my work is how the wave-function structure of noninteracting electrons can shape correlated quantum matter.

## Quantum Geometry — established work

Quantum geometry encodes how Bloch wave functions vary with momentum. For time-reversal-breaking Chern bands, Kähler geometry provides a framework for understanding ideal band geometry and its connection to fractional Chern insulators. Our work generalizes this geometric language to time-reversal-symmetric Kramers pairs: quaternion-Kähler structures and non-Abelian metric–curvature relations offer a starting point for asking analogous questions in systems that preserve time reversal.

Scope note: This is a geometric foundation, not yet a demonstration that a particular interacting phase is stabilized.

**Featured work:** Quaternion-Kähler geometry of time reversal symmetric crystals (2026, arXiv:2608.03178).

## Band Topology — established work

Some band-topological structures depend on the wave functions of both occupied and unoccupied states. In the real Hopf insulator, we characterized delicate three-dimensional topology in four-band systems with spinless PT symmetry, including independent Hopf invariants and associated boundary phenomena. I am also interested in the structure of unconventional topological phase transitions.

**Featured work:** Real Hopf insulator (Physical Review B 108, 125101, 2023).

## Correlated Quantum Matter — broader research interests

I am interested in correlated electronic bands, topological order, symmetry-protected topological phases, and generalized symmetries. In particular, I want to understand whether multiband quantum geometry can help identify routes toward correlated phases that preserve time-reversal symmetry.

Scope note: These are directions I wish to pursue, rather than a claim of established many-body results.

These notes give a short proof that every smooth cubic surface over an algebraically closed field contains exactly 27 lines, using the incidence correspondence between cubic surfaces and lines, and then survey the lattice-theoretic and modern viewpoints.

## Contents

1. Introduction.
2. The geometry of cubic surfaces: the 27 lines on the Fermat cubic and the incidence correspondence as a projective bundle over the Grassmannian.
3. Deformation of lines: a line on a smooth cubic surface has no first-order deformations.
4. Counting the 27 lines: finite étale projection, and the same count 27 as a top Chern class on the Grassmannian (Schubert calculus).
5. The role of the Picard group: blow-ups of six points, intersection numbers of lines, and the E₆ root system.
6. Toward derived and modern perspectives: virtual fundamental classes, derived categories, and the 𝔸¹-enumerative count of Kass and Wickelgren.
7. Conclusion.

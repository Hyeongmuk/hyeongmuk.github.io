This note is a companion to *From the Abelian Higgs Model to the Toric Code*. That note's lattice construction makes three choices without discussion: temporal gauge, Gauss's law imposed on states, and compact link variables. This note examines each choice in the continuum abelian Higgs model, around the guiding question: fixing one gauge condition or two — does it matter, and if not, why does the lattice insist on one?

## Contents

1. Introduction.
2. Constrained dynamics of the abelian Higgs model: the Lagrangian, the two first-class constraints, and temporal gauge.
3. One gauge condition or two: the Coulomb condition and where it drifts, the extended Hamiltonian, the gauge-invariant statement, and the quantum-mechanical equivalence.
4. Why the lattice fixes only one.
5. Unitary gauge and the Higgs phase.
6. Compact gauge group and holonomies: charge quantization, large gauge transformations, and the route to the toric code.
7. Appendix: constraint chains and depth.
